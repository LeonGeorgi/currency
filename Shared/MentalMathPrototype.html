Familiar ways to do the same calculation

These exact rewrites are used by the search, not just shown as tips. Halving and decimal shifts keep one running value. Compensation uses a saved value, which counts toward the memory estimate. This is a design preference, not a measured guarantee of faster calculation for everyone.

Direct multiplication

×3–×10 are allowed as direct instructions. Their effort weights are higher than doubling or shifting the decimal point. ×5 still favors halving then ×10. Division by 3, 6, 7, 8 or 9 is reserved for counter-rules; ÷4 uses two halvings and ÷5 uses doubling then ÷10.

Preferred methods and alternatives

What the search considers

Doubling, halving, direct ×3–×10, decimal shifts, and compensation around 10 or 100 (×9, ×11, ×99, ×101). Decompositions remain available. ×5 prefers halving followed by ×10; shifts are merged and opposing doubles/halves cancel. Percentage corrections are built from halves and tenths; −50% is simply halving. No direct division by 3–9, sixths, eighths, or 12.5% shortcuts in the simple search. Division by 4 and 5 can still be performed through familiar decompositions. At most one percentage correction. The default cap is four instruction-level arithmetic actions, including decimal shifts. Effort is scored separately: doubling, halving and decimal shifts have weight 1; direct ×3, ×4 and ×6–×9 have weight 2; direct ×5 has weight 3 so its two-action decomposition remains preferred. These weights are uncalibrated preferences, not times. A 25% correction counts as two halvings plus addition/subtraction; a 15% correction has four actions and three retained values. Compact labels do not count as single effortless operations.

The default targets 2% error and may accept up to 3% when it lowers the estimated effort score. No independently simple rule is returned when that limit cannot be met with the conservative vocabulary and work budget. Every found rule also gets its exact inverse: reverse the step order and undo each operation. Inverses are shown even outside that vocabulary or error limit, without assigning an uncalibrated difficulty score. Adding 10% does not undo subtracting 10%; multiplying by 10 and then dividing by 9 does. The inverse undoes the approximate rule, not the market conversion, so its market error is calculated separately. Percentages apply to the current result. Do not round between steps.

The vocabulary and work counts are engineering choices informed by arithmetic research, not measured human response times. Actual difficulty still depends on the amount, carries, practice and individual skill. Read the primary research note for studies and limits.
